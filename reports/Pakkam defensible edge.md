# Make Pakkam the answer you can show

Pakkam should commit to one direction: **become the welfare answer a person can carry to the counter.** Every spoken Tamil, Tanglish, Hindi or English answer should end in something she can hold up to the officer, her son or an e-Sevai operator: the official page, with the line highlighted, the name of the person who checked it, the date it was checked, and the next thing she has to do. Call the feature **"Kaattu" (show it)**. It joins three things Pakkam already has, the page citations, the 22 signed fact cards and the `/check-forward` backend, with the WIP "show the officer" page-image endpoint, so the citation stops being a footnote and becomes the product. The evidence for this direction is better than for any feature-level alternative. In the Delhi widow-pension RCT, information alone did nothing for illiterate women, while help with the form closed the literacy gap ([Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/poverty-inequality/who-does-not-get-caught-by-social-safety-nets)). Services for the poor have lasted on accountability and intermediaries, not on technology ([Acumen](https://acumen.org/case-studies/haqdarshak/); [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/)). And at least five rival GIAIC entries already claim "grounded" or "the LLM never decides", but none shows the page and the person who checked it ([Kural](https://github.com/Mirdumurugesan/kural); [JanVaani](https://github.com/amantebriwal4321/janvaani-gnani)). This direction drops the phone line the earlier report ranked first: rivals already have phone lines, so it is no longer an edge. Before any of this, one compliance gap comes first. **The challenge lists Evon v3.3 among the Gnani models entries must use and name, and Pakkam currently runs its answers on Claude/Gemini with Evon "not used"** ([Gnani internship page](https://www.gnani.ai/internship)). Request the Evon weights today.

## The field has caught up on grounding, but not on proof

GIAIC 2026 has no published rubric. It runs as ten separate ₹50,000 awards, one award per team at most, each judged on a single axis. An internship track runs alongside it, and its shortlist starts with *verified organic engagement* on a nominated LinkedIn/X post ([Gnani internship page](https://www.gnani.ai/internship)). Pakkam's real targets are **Real Deal** (most useful real-world problem) and **Mixed Vibes** (code-mixed voice, with Tanglish named). It also has an outside chance at **Demo Day Drop** (best 60-second video). Noise Canceller and Main Character are weak fits: Pakkam's own README says real phone audio and spoken Tanglish are untested ([Pakkam repo](https://github.com/mailtojaswanthss-svg/pakkam)), and Tamil is not low-resource.

The welfare-scheme lane is crowded. Kural is a Tamil scheme helpline on Prisma, Evon and Timbre. It answers only from retrieved passages with the source attached, escalates to a human callback ticket, runs a Twilio line and simulates noisy 8 kHz μ-law audio ([Kural](https://github.com/Mirdumurugesan/kural)). JanVaani covers 19 schemes in 9 languages, uses a deterministic eligibility engine and an 8 kHz softphone, and prints a CSC "Ready Slip" with a QR code. It shows **no source citations and no accuracy numbers** ([JanVaani](https://github.com/amantebriwal4321/janvaani-gnani)). Urimai, Thunai, tn-schemematch and a Tanglish Anganwadi bot are working on the same Tamil Nadu ground ([Urimai](https://github.com/rgokulkrishna44-debug/urimai); [Thunai](https://github.com/murali18-a/Thunai); [tn-schemematch](https://github.com/kanishk5676/tn-schemematch)). A phone line, refusals and "the model doesn't decide" are now table stakes, not edges.

What no rival shows is **provenance down to page and signer, plus a published accuracy record**. Pakkam's final live run gave **97% correct-page citations, 81% of covered questions answered and 92% of out-of-scope questions refused (n=60)**, from a published 194-question benchmark ([LIVE_BENCHMARK.md](../docs/review/LIVE_BENCHMARK.md)). The closest winning precedent points the same way. MEGHA took first prize at Harvard Kennedy's AI for Good hackathon as a voice-first service "grounded in verified official documents" ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/ai-solution-for-india-wins-top-spot-at-harvards-hackathon)). Judging research says narrow projects with one complete path score higher. Judges rank against the entries they just saw, and the winning move is "the sentence you want a judge to repeat to another judge" ([Kurbaitaev, DEV](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). Against Kural and JanVaani, that sentence is about proof.

## Information alone fails the women Pakkam serves; something to show does not

The strongest welfare evidence argues against building a better Q&A bot. In Sarika Gupta's Delhi RCT (>1,200 eligible but unenrolled women, 75 slums), **information alone raised applications 14 points for literate women and had no effect on illiterate women**. Adding form-filling help raised applications 6 points, about 40% over control, and **helped literate and illiterate women equally**. Adding accompaniment to the office raised them 11 points, about 70% over control ([Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/poverty-inequality/who-does-not-get-caught-by-social-safety-nets)). A Q&A bot, including ChatGPT, is the information-only arm. Pakkam cannot walk a woman to the taluk office in four days, but it can give her the next-best thing: a spoken checklist plus an official page she can show, so that someone at the counter can verify the claim instead of dismissing it.

Three design findings shape what Kaattu says and shows. **Teach-back improves comprehension of action steps (self-care, follow-up, medication) even in low-health-literacy patients, but not of abstract rules** ([Fed Pract RCT summary](https://www.mdedge.com/fedprac/article/202182/health-policy/use-and-effectiveness-teach-back-method-patient-education-and/page/0/3)). So Pakkam should ask "so tomorrow you will take your ration card and Aadhaar to the e-Sevai centre?", not quiz eligibility. **Spoken dialog alone left 28% of low-literate users unable to finish a task** that a richer UI completed ([Medhi et al., ToCHI 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). **Voice plus visuals shown at the same time confused illiterate users** ([Medhi & Prasad, WWW 2007](https://www.www2007.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/papers/paper764.pdf)). So the page appears *after* the spoken answer, never on top of it. And intermediated use is the norm: on shared phones, people with digital skills act for those without ([Sambasivan et al., CHI 2010](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=159423)), and husbands often took Kilkari calls meant for women who did not own a phone ([BMJ Global Health](https://gh.bmj.com/content/6/Suppl_5/e008838.full)). The page is what lets a son, an SHG leader or an operator check the answer before acting on it.

ASHABot gives direct evidence on how intermediaries trust these tools. **Health workers trusted the bot more when it said "I don't know"** (16.4% of questions). They tied trust to official endorsement: "whatever the government says is correct" ([arXiv 2409.10913](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10913v3)). Kaattu makes both visible: a refusal that names the official helpline, and an answer that literally shows the government's words.

## What ChatGPT could copy, and what it would not bother to

Be honest on stage: **every Kaattu screen is technically copyable.** A custom GPT or Gemini Gem loaded with the same 18 public PDFs can quote pages, answer in Tamil and refuse when told to. Meta is already becoming state governments' WhatsApp service channel: Andhra Pradesh's Mana Mitra reports 2.5 million citizens reached, and Gujarat plans voice services for rural users ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-meta-whatsapp-llama-ai-e-governance-india/); [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/gujarat-govt-taps-meta-for-ai-led-governance-push-digital-citizen-services-on-whatsapp-533293-2026-05-26)). General models do have measured Indic weaknesses: more hallucination in low-resource Indic languages than in English, and large domain gaps ([arXiv 2504.20022](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20022v2); [BhashaBench, arXiv 2510.25409](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25409v2)). But those gaps shrink with every release and are not a moat. The 18 PDFs are not a moat either, because they are public. Casado's a16z rule applies: "defensibility is not inherent to data itself." The exception is a **trustworthy data custodian** ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/)).

What a general assistant structurally does not offer is **someone who answers for the answer**. That means a named human sign-off on each fact card, a "last verified" date, a published accuracy record on these exact schemes, and a refusal policy that is measured, not just promised. ChatGPT's terms disclaim accuracy, and it publishes no welfare-scheme accuracy audit. The durable services in this space all built moats of that kind: Haqdarshak's agents handle documents and charge a fee for applications ([Acumen](https://acumen.org/case-studies/haqdarshak/)), Kilkari gets its reach from MoHFW embedding, and PxD and ARMMAN win government contracts with outcome evidence ([ARMMAN](https://www.armman.org/mmitra/); [PxD registry](https://registry.precisiondev.org/registry_entry/cumin-experiment/)). Even Digital Green's Farmer.CHAT runs *on* OpenAI models and is built for extension agents rather than farmers ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/digital-green)). The model is a commodity input. Pakkam's long-term position is the verified, accountable answer layer that an e-Sevai operator, an SHG federation or a government WhatsApp bot links to, not a rival to it. Gnani's sovereign, self-hostable stack (Prisma, Timbre, open-weight Evon) is what lets a state department adopt that layer.

The honest caveat is that this moat is a direction, not something Pakkam has today. The 22 cards are signed by the team, not by an official. ASHABot shows that expert-in-the-loop review collapses without incentives: reviewers received 6,915 prompts and produced 19 consensus answers ([arXiv 2409.10913](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10913v3)). The pitch should say "signed by our reviewer", never imply government endorsement, and name sign-off scale as the first thing that breaks.

## Sixty seconds, one sentence, four compliance items

**Identity:** *"Ask Pakkam about a welfare scheme in Tamil, Tanglish, Hindi or English by voice, and it gives you the official page, checked by a named person, to show the officer, or it honestly says it doesn't know."*

**Demo storyline (60 s):** In 0–5 s, a synthetic caller asks in spoken Tanglish, "Magalir Urimai Thogai enakku varala, enna documents venum?", with the identity line on screen. In 5–20 s, Pakkam answers in Timbre Tamil, short answer first, and *then* the Kaattu card slides up: the G.O. page, highlighted line, "verified by [reviewer], [date]". In 20–35 s, the spoken checklist runs one document at a time, and she says the next step back. In 35–45 s, she reads a WhatsApp forward ("₹4,000 from Deepavali"). Pakkam shows the official figure and page and says the forward isn't in the official documents, with the OTP warning. In 45–52 s, an out-of-scope question gets a refusal plus the official helpline. ## Conclusion

The question "what can't ChatGPT copy?" has an uncomfortable answer for a four-day build: no screen is uncopyable. What big assistants will not do is *answer for* a welfare answer. They won't put a named person's sign-off and a published error rate behind a ₹1,000 figure, or give an illiterate woman something an officer will accept. That is the gap the Delhi RCT measured, and it is the one Pakkam is closest to filling.

Kaattu turns the assets Pakkam already has into that promise and makes it visible in sixty seconds. The longer-term path is to sell that accountability to the intermediaries and channels that already reach users, not to compete with them for users.
